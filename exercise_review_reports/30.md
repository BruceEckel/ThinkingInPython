## Findings

### 1. Exercises 3 and 4 require constructing an `ExceptionGroup`, a technique the chapter never shows

- **Severity:** minor
- **Where:** `Chapters/30_Patterns--Observer.md:581-586` (exercise 3) and
  `:587-591` (exercise 4)
- **Exercise says:** "`notify()` re-raises the failures afterward,
  together, as an `ExceptionGroup` (the container
  [Concurrency](19_Techniques--Concurrency.md#structured-concurrency-with-taskgroup)
  introduced)."
- **What actually happens:** Both exercises require writing
  `raise ExceptionGroup("message", failures)` — the two-argument
  constructor. Neither this chapter nor chapter 19 (the chapter the
  exercise links to for `ExceptionGroup`) ever shows that constructor
  call. Chapter 19's `task_group.py` only *catches* an `ExceptionGroup`
  that `asyncio.TaskGroup` builds automatically, via
  `except* ValueError as group: for exc in group.exceptions:`.
  Confirmed with:
  `grep -rn "ExceptionGroup(" Chapters/` returns no hits anywhere in the
  book, and `grep -rln "ExceptionGroup" Chapters/` shows only chapters 19
  and 30 mention the type at all. A reader who has only read the chapter
  (and its one linked section) has seen `ExceptionGroup` consumed, never
  built.
- **Solution does:** `Solutions/30_Patterns--Observer.md` exercise 3 and 4
  both do `raise ExceptionGroup("observer failures", failures)`, which is
  correct Python and runs fine (verified: `uv run python exercise_3.py`
  and `exercise_4.py` both print `1 [7]`, matching their `#:` markers, and
  `test_resilient_notify.py` / `test_async_resilient_notify.py` both
  pass). The solution's code is right; the chapter just never taught the
  constructor it uses.
- **Suggested fix:** Either add a one-line mention/example of
  `ExceptionGroup(message, list_of_exceptions)` somewhere in this chapter
  or chapter 19 before the exercises, or soften the exercise's link text
  to acknowledge the reader will need to look up the constructor (the
  builtin is discoverable and its signature is intuitive, so this is not
  blocking, but it is a fact the chapter asserts it "introduced" without
  actually showing).

- Spot-checked a subtler prose claim in the exercise-4 solution: that
  filtering `results` with `isinstance(r, Exception)` (not
  `BaseException`) matters because `asyncio.CancelledError` derives from
  `BaseException`. Confirmed by direct test
  (`asyncio.gather(broken(), ok(), return_exceptions=True)` where
  `broken()` raises `CancelledError`) that the cancellation does come
  back as an ordinary list element, and separately confirmed
  `ExceptionGroup("x", [CancelledError()])` raises
  `TypeError: Cannot nest BaseExceptions in an ExceptionGroup`. So the
  `Exception`-only filter is not just stylistically safer, it is
  necessary to avoid a crash — a stronger reason than the solution's own
  prose gives ("would swallow a cancellation the event loop meant to
  propagate"), but not a false claim, so not reported as a finding.

  scratch) passed with no errors, and `uv run pytest` on both solution
